# Code of conduct

## What this is

A short statement of how people are expected to treat each other in this
repository, written for it rather than adopted from a template, so that nothing
in it describes a structure this project does not have.

It applies to the issue tracker, pull requests, commit messages, code comments,
documentation and any other space this project uses, and to private contact that
starts because of this project.

## What is expected

Argue with the work. Disagreement about a decision, a measurement or a design is
the point of writing decisions down, and somebody who says a decision is wrong is
doing the thing the decision file exists for.

Say what you measured and what you assumed, and keep them apart. A confident
sentence with nothing behind it costs the next person their afternoon.

Take a correction as information. Being wrong in public is the ordinary cost of
working in public, and a repository where nobody is ever wrong is one where
nobody is checking.

Assume the other person is doing their best with what they had. Most of what
looks like carelessness is missing context, and asking is cheaper than
concluding.

Leave people the option to disengage. Nobody here owes anybody a reply, a review
or a fix, and a request that is not answered is not an insult.

## What is not acceptable

Attacking the person rather than the work. That includes the polite version,
where the sentence is about somebody's competence, motive or seriousness rather
than about a claim they made.

Harassment of any kind, in the open or in private, including unwanted contact
that continues after somebody has asked for it to stop.

Insults or dismissals based on who somebody is, which covers age, disability,
appearance, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression,
experience level, education, socioeconomic status, nationality, personal
appearance, race, caste, colour, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

Publishing somebody's private information, including a physical or electronic
address, without their explicit permission. This project has a specific reason to
name that: what this program is configured with is where somebody lives and when
they sleep. Do not paste another operator's configuration anywhere, and strip
your own before you share it.

Deliberate disruption. Repeatedly reopening a settled decision without new
evidence, filing noise, or arguing in bad faith to exhaust the other side.

Sexual attention or advances of any kind. This is a repository about a telescope
alert stream.

## Reporting

Reports go to nils.lehnen@proton.me, which is the maintainer, and which is the
address published on the maintainer's GitHub profile.

There is no committee and there is nobody else to escalate to. That is a real
limitation and it is written here rather than left to be discovered. If your
report is about the maintainer, GitHub's own abuse reporting is the route that
does not go through the person it is about, and it is at
https://github.com/contact/report-abuse.

A report should say what happened and where, with links where there are links. A
report that arrives is acknowledged. What is done about it is not published
unless the person who reported it wants it published, and nothing about a
reporter is shared with the person reported without asking first.

## What happens after a report

The maintainer reads it, asks for anything that is missing, and decides. The
responses available, roughly in order:

A private word saying what was wrong and why, with no public trace.

A public correction, where the thing happened in public and leaving it uncorrected
would look like agreement.

Removing or editing a comment, a commit message or an issue body, saying that it
was removed and why.

A temporary block from the repository, for a stated period, with the condition
for coming back.

A permanent block.

The reason for whatever is chosen is written down and given to the person it
applies to. Somebody who thinks a decision was wrong can say so, to the
maintainer or through the abuse route above.

## Scope, honestly stated

This is one person's judgement, applied to a small repository. It is not a
process with appeals, timelines or a second reader, and pretending otherwise
would be the kind of overclaiming this project tries to avoid everywhere else.
[GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md) says the same thing about decisions generally and
about what happens if that person stops.
